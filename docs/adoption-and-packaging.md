# Adoption, packaging and expansion

Status: proposed commercial design, 2026-09-05. No prices, entitlements, free-credit
allowances or conversion forecasts are approved by this document. See the
[roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) and [service contract](studio-connected-services.md).

## Value proposition

**Press is complete for my image job. Studio adds capability for our product
business.** Supplier preparation gives the app a concrete entry point; reusable
local product-image work gives people a reason to keep it after that submission.

Do not measure success by forcing browser visits. A customer using paid Studio
services from Press is using Studio. A supplier who never pays directly can still
create value for the retailer by reducing corrections and support work. Both are
hypotheses to test with operational evidence, not assumed conversion effects.

## Proposed packaging boundary

| Experience | Proposed inclusion | Proposed payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local workbench | Existing local operations, reusable presets, supported checks, product grouping, local outputs and reports | No account or purchase required for ordinary local work |
| Retailer-requested supplier work | Assignment/requirement access and authorized submission, status and correction | Retailer's contracted portal service; no separate supplier purchase just to comply |
| Optional hosted image operations | Explicit selected inputs, quoted service usage and recovery | User's selected authorized Studio workspace |
| Shared product workspace | Cloud assets, collaborative review, version context and managed delivery as supported | Workspace subscription/service arrangement |
| Sponsored corrections, later | Restricted allowance for approved suppliers/tasks, with budget and receipts | Consenting retailer under a separate server-enforced grant |

This is a packaging proposal, not an entitlement bypass. Server plans, authorization,
quotas and the secure native-client contract must be reconciled before rollout.
Supplier and paid AI access are distinct; a current general API-plan gate must not
be accidentally inherited as a requirement to buy a personal plan for submission.

Avoid artificial local file-count limits, delayed local processing, watermarks,
account-required preset sharing or deliberately inferior local model output. Model
licensing, hardware constraints and honest supported-format limits still apply.
Do not imply that every hosted model is better; demonstrate task-specific value.

Start by charging for actual hosted compute and persistent shared services. Leave
standalone desktop subscriptions, template marketplaces and bundled sponsor-credit
promotions out of the first pilot. Decide prices only after cost and value evidence.

## Retailer-led route

Introduce Press at a relevant point in the supplier invitation, requirements view,
or repeated-batch workflow: **Prepare this batch in Press** beside the browser path.
Explain local versus cloud work and provide a clear install/return journey. Never
require installation for occasional suppliers or promise IT acceptance on untested
platforms. Respect managed-device restrictions; preserve browser/SFTP paths where
already available rather than treating desktop adoption as compulsory.

Resolve the invited task after sign-in. The link is not a permission token or a
command to submit files. A retailer pilot should include setup by somebody other
than the principal developer so support requirements are visible.

The commercial hypothesis is reduced correction rounds and operator effort, not
retailer profit from upselling their suppliers. Do not interrupt a blocked supplier
with unrelated offers for the supplier's own workspace. First help finish the
retailer's job using permitted local and portal capabilities.

## Supplier-led route

Allow independent website and marketplace preparation without an account. Make it
possible to reuse the same master and personal recipe for other jobs without leaking
retailer-specific metadata or permissions. Exporting a personal preset is useful on
its own; it should not be a concealed lead-capture form.

Offer the next service only in context: a chosen hosted repair, a lifestyle candidate,
a request for another person's review, or a need for a shared master library.
Connecting the supplier's own workspace must be explicit and should preserve the
local job. Do not copy retailer data, import their contacts, or solicit other suppliers
on the user's behalf. Templates and selected job links convey data, not membership.

A supplier may later become a Studio customer for their own team or incoming assets.
Test this as an expansion hypothesis; it is not an automatic network effect or a
reason to gate ordinary submission behind an upgrade.

## Contextual offers and respect for a no

Each offer should identify the current problem, additional capability, cloud boundary
and likely charging model before execution. Keep **Continue locally** available when
possible, and **Use another image / get a new photograph** when AI is not a sound fix.
If the user dismisses an offer, suppress it for that task/context; do not reset the
promotion on every selection change or successful export.

Use neutral capability language, not unsupported quality superiority. No countdowns,
fake urgency, unexpected recurring purchases or preselected sponsorship. No automatic
free-credit enrollment. A successful generated image is still inspected for product
accuracy and is not guaranteed to meet the recipient's policy.

## Pilot and measures

Start with one consenting retailer and approximately five to ten active suppliers.
Observe two representative jobs per supplier where practical, recording the previous
browser-only process for comparison. This is a small observational pilot, not a
statistically powered experiment. Agree task and improvement thresholds before
collecting results; do not select a favorable threshold after seeing the data.

| Question | Measure and denominator |
| --- | --- |
| Can suppliers complete the task? | Suppliers reaching a reviewable first submission without live help / suppliers attempting their first real job |
| Is quality improved? | First-pass technically accepted items / items with completed authoritative validation; keep human rejection reasons separate |
| Is effort reduced? | Active preparation time, correction rounds per completed product set and support minutes per supplier/job, compared on similar tasks |
| Does it retain utility? | Suppliers completing another real job / activated suppliers who had another job during the observation period |
| Does hosted processing add value? | Users keeping a completed hosted candidate and returning for paid use / eligible users choosing the service; distinguish sponsored from self-paid |
| Is it economical? | Net service revenue less provider, retry, transfer/storage, refund and support costs per completed paid job |
| Does the retailer benefit without supplier purchases? | Operator time and resubmission burden before/after, together with continued retailer use |

Downloads, installs, gross credits consumed and web visits are diagnostic counts,
not the north-star outcome. Use completed, correctly scoped deliverables/submissions
and repeat real jobs as the primary evidence. Separate retry traffic from new jobs;
separate one free trial from a retained paying workspace. Never count a transport
success as approval or pending validation as a technical pass.

Instrument connected operations from necessary server receipts, with data-minimized
client context. Optional local usage research requires consent and an off switch;
local work does not need mandatory behavioral telemetry. Do not send raw paths,
filenames, SKUs, prompts or private contact lists for product analytics. Use explicit
consented interview/observation records when those details are needed for support.

## Rollout, economics and decision gates

First validate supplier completion and correctness without new sponsored-AI offers.
Then expose one contextual self-paid service to a consenting subset; observe actual
useful outcomes, spend surprises, refunds and support burden. Only then consider
retailer-funded correction budgets or a broader service bundle. This ordering keeps
pricing experiments from obscuring whether the underlying workflow works.

Before each paid pilot, name a budget owner, cost ceiling, allowed operations,
retention policy, refund/cancellation policy and support escalation path. Track
provider retries and orphan-reservation recovery as real costs. Do not invent a
fixed gross-margin target here; the service owner must set and approve one against
measured operating costs before widening the offer.

Any wrong-tenant/product delivery, unauthorized charge, silent upload, source loss,
or unrecoverable accepted job blocks expansion. Pause the implicated integration or
operation, retain receipts and local work, and return users to the existing browser
or local path. Do not shut off the useful offline product as a billing kill switch.

Expand only when users complete repeat work and the added service solves a documented
problem at acceptable support cost. If download friction dominates, improve the web
handoff or browser workflow. If local processing meets the need, retain that success
instead of manufacturing another reason to purchase Studio.
